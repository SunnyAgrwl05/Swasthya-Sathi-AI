# Childhood Illness & IMNCI Protocols — Child Survival Guidelines

## Diarrheal Disease & Dehydration Management
Diarrhea is a leading cause of under-5 mortality. Immediate intervention with ORS and Zinc is lifesaving:

### 1. Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)
- **Preparation**: Dissolve the entire 20.5-gram WHO-formula ORS packet into exactly 1 liter of clean drinking water. Mix well until completely dissolved. Discard unused solution after 24 hours.
- **Administration Guidelines**:
  - Children under 2 years: 50 to 100 ml of ORS after each loose stool.
  - Children 2 to 10 years: 100 to 200 ml of ORS after each loose stool.
  - Older children and adults: As much as they want.

### 2. Zinc Supplementation (Essential 14-Day Therapy)
- **Clinical Benefit**: Reduces duration and severity of the current episode, and protects against recurrent diarrhea episodes for up to 3 months.
- **Dosage**:
  - Infants 2 months to 6 months: 10 mg elemental zinc daily for 14 continuous days (even after diarrhea stops).
  - Children 6 months to 5 years: 20 mg elemental zinc daily for 14 continuous days.
- **Administration**: Disperse the tablet in a teaspoon of breast milk or clean water.

### 3. Dehydration Signs & Referral
- **No Dehydration**: Child is active and alert, drinks normally, skin pinch goes back immediately. -> Home management with ORS, Zinc, and continued feeding.
- **Some Dehydration**: Restless, irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly/thirsty, skin pinch goes back slowly. -> Treat with ORS at health facility or sub-center for 4 hours; if not improving, refer to PHC.
- **Severe Dehydration**: Lethargic or unconscious, sunken eyes, unable to drink or drinking poorly, skin pinch goes back very slowly (> 2 seconds). -> **Medical Emergency**: Immediate IV Ringer's Lactate and urgent transfer to PHC/hospital.

## Acute Respiratory Infections (ARI) & Pneumonia
Pneumonia is identified in field settings by counting respiratory rates when the child is calm:

### Fast Breathing Thresholds:
- **Age < 2 months**: 60 breaths per minute or more.
- **Age 2 months to 11 months**: 50 breaths per minute or more.
- **Age 12 months to 5 years**: 40 breaths per minute or more.

### Clinical Classification & Action:
1. **No Pneumonia (Cough or Cold)**:
   - Breathing rate is normal, no chest indrawing.
   - Action: Home care, soothing warm liquids (lemon/ginger tea with jaggery or warm water), continue breastfeeding, no antibiotics required. Counsel mother on danger signs.
2. **Pneumonia (Fast Breathing)**:
   - Fast breathing present, but NO chest indrawing and NO general danger signs.
   - Action: Refer to ANM or PHC Medical Officer for oral antibiotic prescription (e.g., Amoxicillin as per protocol).
3. **Severe Pneumonia**:
   - Chest indrawing (lower chest wall moves inward on breathing in), stridor in calm child, or inability to feed.
   - Action: Immediate pre-referral dose of oral amoxicillin (if trained) and **urgent hospital referral**.

## General Danger Signs in Children Under 5
Any child with one or more of these signs must be rushed to the PHC immediately:
- Inability to drink or breastfeed.
- Vomiting everything ingested.
- Convulsions or seizures during the current illness.
- Lethargic, drowsy, or unconscious.
- Severe malnutrition (visible severe wasting or bilateral pitting edema).
